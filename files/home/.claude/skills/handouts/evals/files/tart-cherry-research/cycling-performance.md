# Research: tart cherry supplementation for cycling performance/recovery and repeated-day endurance

## Summary
Cycling-specific tart-cherry evidence is small: one acute trained-cyclist performance study supports a single pre-exercise Montmorency tart cherry concentrate dose, and one trained-cyclist 3-day stochastic/stage-race simulation supports lower inflammation/oxidative-stress markers but not a clear performance claim. Stage-race-relevant protocols generally use either an acute **60 mL** concentrate dose about **1.5 h pre-exercise** or a short loading block of **30 mL concentrate twice daily** spanning the days before and during competition.

## Findings
1. **Cycling-specific performance evidence: acute pre-exercise dosing in trained cyclists.** Keane et al. studied Montmorency tart cherry concentrate and exercise performance in trained cyclists. The performance protocol used a **single 60 mL dose of Montmorency tart cherry concentrate about 1.5 h before a 15-km cycling time trial**; the paper reports improved time-trial performance alongside increased nitric-oxide-related biomarkers. This is the cleanest cycling-specific source for **acute pre-ride timing**. [Keane et al., *Scand J Med Sci Sports*](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.13088)

2. **Stage-race/high-volume cycling protocol: trained cyclists, 3 consecutive days.** Bell et al. randomized **16 trained cyclists** to Montmorency cherry concentrate or placebo before a **3-day high-intensity stochastic cycling protocol** designed to mimic road-race demands. Dose was **30 mL concentrate twice daily** (**60 mL/day**, typically diluted) for **7 consecutive days total**, spanning the pre-load and the 3 repeated cycling days. Main finding: tart cherry attenuated oxidative-stress/inflammatory responses, but this was **not a free-paced race-performance trial**, so it should be interpreted as recovery-biomarker support rather than proof of stage-race performance enhancement. [Bell et al., *Nutrients* 2014](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu6020829)

3. **Meta-analysis: endurance benefit is plausible but based on few heterogeneous studies.** Gao & Chilibeck's endurance meta-analysis concluded tart cherry concentrate can improve endurance performance overall, with included protocols ranging from multi-day loading to acute pre-exercise use; the authors specifically note effective use windows from roughly **7 days before to 1.5 h before exercise**. This supports the practical timing window but does not remove the limitation that cycling-specific trials are sparse. [Gao & Chilibeck 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32040340/)

4. **Repeated-day endurance/recovery protocol relevant to stage racing: relay running.** Kuehl et al. used tart cherry juice in runners before the Hood-to-Coast relay: **355 mL tart cherry juice twice daily** for **7 days before** and **on race day**. It reduced post-race muscle pain versus placebo, but it was a running relay study and did not establish cycling power or stage-race time gains. [Kuehl et al., *JISSN* 2010](https://doi.org/10.1186/1550-2783-7-17)

5. **Post-endurance recovery protocol: marathon loading plus 48 h after.** Howatson et al. used tart cherry juice around marathon running: **two 8-oz/237-mL servings per day** for **5 days before**, **race day**, and **48 h after**. Recovery markers improved, including faster recovery of muscle function and lower inflammation/oxidative stress markers, but this was not cycling and did not demonstrate a race-time benefit. [Howatson et al., *Scand J Med Sci Sports*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19883392/)

6. **Powder protocol sometimes used for endurance events.** Levers et al. used a powdered Montmorency tart cherry supplement in aerobically trained runners: **480 mg/day** for **10 days** — **7 days pre-race, race day, and 2 days post-race**; race-day dosing was taken before the half-marathon. The study reported faster half-marathon performance and improved recovery/inflammation markers, but translation to cycling is indirect. [Levers et al., *JISSN* 2016](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-016-0133-z)

7. **Null/neutral evidence matters.** The cycling stage-race simulation is positive for biomarkers but **neutral/insufficient for performance**, because workload was controlled rather than a time trial/race outcome. Marathon and relay studies are similarly strongest for soreness/recovery markers, not performance. Reviews also report mixed results for creatine kinase, soreness, and performance depending on exercise mode, dose, training status, and whether the exercise causes meaningful muscle damage. [Vitale et al. review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=Tart+Cherry+Supplementation+and+Recovery+From+Strenuous+Exercise%3A+A+Systematic+Review+and+Meta-Analysis)

8. **Practical cycling takeaways from the evidence.** For an A-race/time trial, the most cycling-specific acute protocol is **60 mL Montmorency concentrate ~90 min pre-exercise**. For a stage-race/high-volume block, the most cycling-specific protocol is **30 mL twice daily for 7 consecutive days total**, started before the first hard day and continued during the repeated-day block. For recovery after unusually damaging endurance events, comparable non-cycling protocols continue supplementation for **48 h post-event**. Treat this as an event-targeted intervention, not a year-round training supplement, because high-dose antioxidant/polyphenol strategies may be unnecessary during normal adaptation-focused training.

- Dropped: tart-cherry supplement marketing pages — useful for product claims but not independent evidence.
- Dropped: broad cherry-health reviews and hypertension/vascular-function-only studies — useful mechanistically, but not cycling/repeated-day endurance performance or recovery trials.
- Dropped: resistance-exercise-only studies — informative for muscle damage, but less applicable to cycling because cycling is mostly concentric and typically causes less eccentric muscle damage.

## Gaps
- There are very few trained-cyclist trials, and only one clearly stage-race-like cycling protocol was found; it mainly reports biomarkers, not race outcomes.
- Exact anthocyanin/polyphenol content varies widely by product and batch; matching by “30 mL concentrate” may not match active compound exposure across brands.
- More useful next-step evidence would be a randomized crossover trial in trained cyclists during a real or simulated multi-day stage race with daily TT/critical-power outcomes, GI tolerance, sleep, soreness, and inflammatory markers.
