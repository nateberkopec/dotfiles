# Research: Tart cherry juice/concentrate for exercise recovery in endurance athletes and cyclists

## Summary
Tart cherry (usually Montmorency) has **low-to-moderate quality evidence** for modestly improving recovery after muscle-damaging endurance exercise: less soreness/pain in some running trials and faster restoration of strength/power in several small RCTs. The most consistent protocol is **pre-loading plus post-event continuation**: tart cherry concentrate **30 mL twice daily** or juice **~240-355 mL twice daily**, started **4-7 days before** the event and continued through **48-72 h after**; CK effects are inconsistent, while CRP/IL-6/oxidative-stress markers more often improve.

## Findings
1. **Best-supported timing is a loading protocol, not “drink it after the workout.”** Most positive athlete studies dosed tart cherry before the damaging event and continued after it: juice ~8-12 fl oz twice daily, concentrate 30 mL twice daily, or a proprietary powder for ~7 days pre-event plus 2 days post-event. [Vitale 2017 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28696985/), [Howatson 2010 marathon RCT](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19883392/), [Bell 2014 cycling RCT](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/6/2/829)
2. **Endurance-running RCTs show the clearest practical signals for pain and strength recovery.** In a 54-runner relay RCT, tart cherry juice reduced post-race pain increase versus placebo after ~7 days of BID juice; in a 20-runner marathon RCT, tart cherry juice improved post-marathon isometric strength recovery and attenuated inflammation/oxidative stress. [Kuehl 2010](https://jissn.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1550-2783-7-17), [Howatson 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19883392/)
3. **Cyclist-specific recovery evidence is sparse but favorable for inflammation/oxidative stress during repeated hard days.** The key cycling RCT used Montmorency concentrate 30 mL twice daily across a 7-day protocol around 3 consecutive days of high-intensity stochastic cycling; it reported lower inflammatory/oxidative-stress responses, but did not provide strong direct evidence for DOMS or next-day power recovery. [Bell 2014](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/6/2/829)
4. **Functional recovery signals are stronger than CK signals.** Small RCTs in muscle-damaging exercise more consistently show improved maximum voluntary contraction, jump/strength recovery, or perceived soreness than reductions in creatine kinase. CK is biologically noisy and often unchanged despite improved function. [Bowtell 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21233776/), [Clifford 2016](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/8/7/441), [Connolly 2006](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/40/8/679)
5. **Evidence quality: promising but not definitive.** Trials are randomized and usually placebo-controlled, but most are small, short, product-specific, and male-heavy; several use multiple biomarkers with limited correction and some involve proprietary products/funding. A broader Cochrane review of antioxidant supplements found only small, low-certainty DOMS effects overall, so tart cherry should be treated as an optional A-race/recovery-block tool rather than a core intervention. [Cochrane antioxidant DOMS review](https://www.cochrane.org/CD009789/MUSKINJ_antioxidants-preventing-and-reducing-muscle-soreness-after-exercise)

## Randomized trial protocols and outcomes

Abbreviations: **BID** = twice daily; **MTC** = Montmorency tart cherry concentrate; **DOMS** = delayed-onset muscle soreness; **MVC** = maximum voluntary contraction; **CK** = creatine kinase; **CRP** = C-reactive protein.

| Study | Population / exercise | Exact dosing & timing reported | Main recovery outcomes | Evidence quality note |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| [Kuehl 2010, JISSN](https://jissn.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1550-2783-7-17) | 54 runners; long-distance relay running event | Tart cherry juice **355 mL BID** for **7 days before** and on/around race period vs placebo | Smaller increase in post-run muscle pain/DOMS; no strength, CK, or inflammatory-marker endpoints | Best endurance sample size among recovery trials, but subjective pain endpoint and recreational-event design |
| [Howatson 2010, Scand J Med Sci Sports](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19883392/) | 20 recreational marathon runners | Tart cherry juice, **2 servings/day** for **5 days pre-marathon, race day, and 48 h post** vs placebo | Faster recovery of isometric knee-extensor strength; lower inflammation/oxidative stress; CK/muscle-damage markers not consistently improved | Direct endurance-event RCT, but small n and mixed biomarkers |
| [Bell 2014, Nutrients](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/6/2/829) | Trained cyclists; 3 consecutive days high-intensity stochastic cycling | MTC **30 mL BID** for **7 days total**, covering pre-load and the cycling protocol, vs placebo | Attenuated inflammatory and oxidative-stress responses across repeated cycling days; limited direct soreness/power-recovery data | Most relevant cyclist trial; small n and marker-focused |
| [Levers 2016, JISSN](https://jissn.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12970-016-0133-z) | Endurance-trained runners/triathletes; half-marathon | Freeze-dried Montmorency tart cherry powder **480 mg/day** for **10 days**: 7 days pre-race, race day, 2 days post | Faster race times plus lower post-race inflammatory/catabolic stress and soreness measures | Relevant endurance athletes; proprietary powder equivalence to juice/concentrate uncertain |
| [Bowtell 2011, Med Sci Sports Exerc](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21233776/) | Trained men; intensive unilateral leg-extension muscle damage | MTC **30 mL BID** for about **10 days**: pre-load plus 48 h recovery window vs placebo | Improved MVC/strength recovery after damaging exercise; CK/muscle-damage biomarkers mixed | Strong functional endpoint and crossover-style design, but n≈10 and not endurance-specific |
| [Clifford 2016, Nutrients](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/8/7/441) | Prolonged intermittent shuttle-running model | MTC **30 mL BID** for **8 days**: 4 days pre, exercise day, 3 days post vs placebo | Better recovery of muscle function/power and lower inflammatory/oxidative-stress markers; CK not a consistent decisive signal | Useful for repeated-sprint/team-sport recovery; not cycling/endurance-specific |
| [Connolly 2006, BJSM](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/40/8/679) | Eccentric elbow-flexor muscle-damage model | Tart cherry juice blend **12 fl oz BID** for **8 days**, with damaging exercise mid-protocol | Less strength loss and some soreness/pain benefit versus placebo | Foundational RCT; small, non-endurance, upper-body model |

## Practical dosing/timing synthesis

- **Concentrate:** most common modern protocol is **30 mL Montmorency tart cherry concentrate twice daily** (total **60 mL/day**), often diluted in water. Start **4-7 days before** the target race/block and continue through **48-72 h after**.
- **Juice:** trial doses range from **~8-12 fl oz / 240-355 mL twice daily** (roughly **480-710 mL/day**), usually for **5-7 days pre-event** and **race day + 2 days post**.
- **Powder:** one endurance RCT used **480 mg/day** freeze-dried Montmorency tart cherry powder for **10 days** (7 pre, race day, 2 post). Powder products are less interchangeable because anthocyanin/polyphenol content varies.
- **Use-case:** most defensible for an A-race, hilly/downhill run, stage race, heavy training camp, or repeated hard cycling days where rapid recovery matters. Less evidence for routine daily use throughout normal training.
- **What to monitor:** GI tolerance, added carbohydrate/sugar load, sleep effects, and whether high antioxidant/polyphenol use is being reserved for competition/recovery blocks rather than every adaptation-focused session.

## Gaps
- Very few RCTs are **cyclist-specific** and even fewer measure next-day power, repeated time-trial recovery, DOMS, and CK together.
- No robust dose-response trials define the minimum effective anthocyanin/polyphenol dose; “30 mL concentrate” and “480 mg powder” are product-specific.
- Female endurance athletes, masters athletes, elite cyclists, multi-day stage races, and long-term training-adaptation effects are under-studied.
- CK should not be used as the main decision metric; it is too variable and is often discordant with function and soreness.
