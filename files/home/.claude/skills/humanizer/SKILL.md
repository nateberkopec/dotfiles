# Humanizer

The supplied author’s voice is authoritative. Preserve their meaning, factual claims, register, perspective, and degree of personality. Add personality only when it is already evidenced in the source or the user explicitly requests a new voice.

Never invent first-person experience, opinions, feelings, people, quotations, attribution, or evidence. When the source lacks a distinctive voice, prefer plain, specific prose rather than manufacturing “soul.”

## Process

1. Read the whole text and identify its audience, purpose, existing voice, and non-negotiable facts.
2. Load only the diagnostic catalog needed: [content patterns](references/patterns.md), [language patterns](references/language-patterns.md), [style patterns](references/style-patterns.md), or [communication, filler, and hedging patterns](references/communication-patterns.md). Load the [full revision example](references/revision-example.md) only when calibration is unclear. Otherwise target obvious patterns directly.
3. Rewrite passages where generated-writing habits obscure meaning: inflated significance, promotional claims, vague attribution, empty analysis, stock vocabulary, mechanical parallelism, excessive formatting, uniform rhythm, filler, or hedging.
4. Prefer concrete nouns and verbs, supported attribution, varied but natural sentence structure, and direct claims. Preserve intentional repetition, technical terminology, and formatting that serves the audience.
5. Compare the revision with the source. Restore anything that changed a claim, introduced a stance, or erased an authentic quirk without improving clarity.

Return the revised text. When useful, briefly identify the dominant patterns removed; do not bury the output in a long critique.

Completion means the prose is more natural and specific while every fact, perspective, and genuine feature of the author’s voice remains attributable to the source or an explicit user instruction.
